### Sample 1: Post-CABG (Cardiothoracic Surgery / Cardiology)
```text
DISCHARGE SUMMARY
Patient: Rajesh Sharma | 62M | IPD #CTVS-88219
Admitted: 10/10/2026 | Discharged: 16/10/2026
Consultant: Dr. Priya Nair, CTVS

PRIMARY DIAGNOSIS:
Status post Coronary Artery Bypass Grafting (CABG x 3: LIMA-LAD, SVG-OM, SVG-RCA) for Severe Triple Vessel Disease (CAD-TVD), stable post-operative course.

PAST MEDICAL HISTORY:
- Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus x 12 years on oral hypoglycemics (HbA1c: 7.8%)
- Essential Hypertension x 8 years
- Dyslipidemia

DISCHARGE MEDICATIONS:
1. Tab Aspirin 75mg once daily after breakfast
2. Tab Clopidogrel 75mg once daily with lunch
3. Tab Atorvastatin 40mg once daily at bedtime
4. Tab Metoprolol succinate 25mg once daily morning (target resting HR 60-70 bpm)
5. Tab Metformin 500mg twice daily with meals
6. Tab Pantoprazole 40mg once daily before breakfast x 14 days

ALLERGIES:
No Known Drug Allergies (NKDA).

RISK FACTORS:
Diabetic foot / impaired wound healing risk, post-sternotomy sternal stability precautions, past tobacco smoker (cessation 2 months prior to surgery).

FOLLOW-UP & POST-DISCHARGE PLAN:
1. Sternal wound dressing and surgical staple removal in CTVS OPD on Day 7 post-discharge.
2. 2D-Echocardiogram and baseline lipid panel in 4 weeks.
3. Daily blood sugar charting (Fasting target < 120 mg/dL).
4. RED FLAGS: Immediately alert hospital or visit Emergency if experiencing sternal clicking/instability, fever > 100.4°F, wound drainage or redness, or acute shortness of breath.
```

### Sample 2: Acute Decompensated Heart Failure (Cardiology)
```text
INPATIENT CLINICAL DISCHARGE NOTE
Patient: Mary Varghese | 71F | IPD #CAR-49102
Admitted: 08/10/2026 | Discharged: 15/10/2026
Department: Cardiology

PRIMARY DIAGNOSIS:
Acute Decompensated Heart Failure with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF, baseline Echo EF 32%), resolved pulmonary congestion and peripheral edema.

COMORBIDITIES:
- Dilated Cardiomyopathy (ischemic etiology)
- Chronic Kidney Disease Stage 3a (baseline serum Cr 1.6 mg/dL)
- Mild pulmonary arterial hypertension

DISCHARGE MEDICATIONS:
- Tab Furosemide (Lasix) 40mg twice daily (8:00 AM & 2:00 PM)
- Tab Sacubitril / Valsartan (Entresto) 24/26mg twice daily
- Tab Spironolactone 25mg once daily morning
- Tab Empagliflozin 10mg once daily morning
- Tab Carvedilol 6.25mg twice daily with meals

ALLERGIES:
Penicillin (history of severe urticarial rash and angioedema).

CLINICAL RISKS & MONITORING:
- High 30-day readmission vulnerability
- Electrolyte shifts / hyperkalemia risk
- Fluid retention / orthopnea recurrence

FOLLOW-UP INSTRUCTIONS:
- Review in Heart Failure Clinic in 5 days with repeat Serum Potassium, Sodium, and Serum Creatinine.
- Strict 1.5 Liters/day fluid restriction.
- Low-sodium diet (< 2g sodium/day).
- Daily morning dry weight after urination: Notify care team immediately if weight gains > 1.5 kg over 48 hours or if nocturnal cough develops.
```

### Sample 3: Total Hip Replacement (Orthopaedics)
```text
SURGICAL DISCHARGE SHEET
Patient: Sundararajan K | 58M | IPD #ORTHO-33918
Admitted: 11/10/2026 | Discharged: 16/10/2026
Department: Orthopaedics

PRIMARY DIAGNOSIS:
End-stage Right Hip Osteoarthritis, status post Right Uncemented Total Hip Arthroplasty (Ceramic-on-polyethylene bearing).

PAST MEDICAL HISTORY:
- Primary Osteoarthritis bilaterally
- Mild bronchial asthma (well-controlled on PRN salbutamol)

DISCHARGE MEDICATIONS:
1. Inj. Enoxaparin 40mg subcutaneous once daily for 14 days (anticoagulation / DVT prophylaxis)
2. Tab Paracetamol 1000mg thrice daily for pain control
3. Tab Tramadol 50mg SOS for breakthrough surgical site pain
4. Tab Pantoprazole 40mg once daily before breakfast
5. Tab Calcium Carbonate + Vitamin D3 once daily

ALLERGIES:
Sulfa drugs (causes facial edema and pruritus).

SURGICAL PRECAUTIONS & RISKS:
- Deep Vein Thrombosis (DVT) / Pulmonary Embolism risk
- Posterior hip dislocation precautions: No hip flexion beyond 90 degrees, do not cross legs, use elevated toilet seat.
- Post-operative fall risk.

FOLLOW-UP:
- Orthopaedic OPD consultation on Day 12 for wound inspection and staple removal.
- Daily walker-assisted gait training with physiotherapist.
- RED FLAGS: Report immediately if sudden calf swelling or pain, chest pain, or surgical wound discharge.
```
